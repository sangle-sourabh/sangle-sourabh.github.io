Vibration, NVH, and structural dynamics

Attenuating the frequency response of free-free beam vibrations using an additively manufactured tuned mass damper

Result50% reduction in vibration response at the targeted modes.

A tuned mass damper is a small mass on a spring that is tuned to a structure’s resonance. It absorbs vibration energy and pulls the resonant peak down. This project designs and tests tuned mass dampers made with additive manufacturing and applies them to a free-free beam.

Try it: a tuned mass damper

Frequency response of a single vibrating structure near resonance. The dashed line is the structure without an absorber.

4% of structure mass · peak reduced by 0 dB

The plot above shows the idea with a simple model. It is a classic single-mode structure with a damper whose tuning and damping follow the standard optimum for a given mass ratio. Drag the slider to see how much peak reduction a small added mass can buy.

What I did

  • Engineered additively manufactured tuned mass dampers, using analytical models to target modes and guide the hardware design.
  • Validated the models with modal tests using a shaker, a single-point laser Doppler vibrometer, and accelerometers.
  • Measured natural frequencies with an NI DAQ and mobility responses with Crystal Instruments SpiderX DAQs and force transducers.

Tools and methods

  • MATLAB models
  • Shaker, single-point LDV, accelerometers, force transducers
  • NI and Crystal Instruments SpiderX DAQs
  • Additive manufacturing
